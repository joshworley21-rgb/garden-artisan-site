# Writing Rules for AI Agents

If you're writing copy for this site, these rules are non-negotiable. They keep the voice grounded, practical, and human—like explaining a trade job to a friend over coffee.

---

## Banned AI Clichés

Never use these words or phrases. They scream "AI-generated content":

- delve
- unlock / unleash
- game-changing / game-changer
- testament
- landscape (unless referring to actual landscape gardening)
- beacon
- tapestry
- furthermore
- in conclusion
- it is important to note
- revolutionise / revolutionary
- transform / transformative
- elevate / elevated
- journey (unless literally travelling)
- passionate / passion
- innovative / innovation
- solutions / solution-driven
- excited to announce
- cutting-edge / leading-edge
- seamless / seamlessly
- leverage
- synergy
- best-in-class / world-class
- premium experience
- unlock potential
- next-level
- empower

## Banned Sentence Patterns

### No Sweeping Openers
❌ "In today's fast-paced world..."  
❌ "In an era where..."  
❌ "As we navigate the complexities of..."  
❌ "When it comes to..."

Just start with the actual point.

### No Em-Dashes for Unnecessary Drama
❌ "Our gardens are beautiful—and sustainable—and affordable."

Use em-dashes sparingly, only when you'd naturally pause in conversation.

✅ "Knowing when it should be cut, and that the roses next to it want pruning at a different point in the year—that's the bit you train for."

### No Repetitive Lists of Three
❌ "We're reliable, professional, and experienced."  
❌ "Fast, affordable, and high-quality."

If you need a list, vary the rhythm. Mix short and long items. Or just pick one strong point and explain it properly.

### No Three-Step Wrap-Up Conclusions
❌ "So there you have it—expert care, honest advice, and a tidy garden. Get in touch today and let's transform your outdoor space together."  
❌ "In summary, we offer quality, reliability, and peace of mind."

These neat three-part endings scream AI. If you've made your point, just stop.

### No Diplomatic Hedging or Filler Bridges
❌ "look no further"  
❌ "rest assured"  
❌ "you can count on us to"  
❌ "we pride ourselves on"  
❌ "committed to excellence"  
❌ "your trusted partner"

Just say the thing. No corporate padding.

### Never Summarize What You Just Wrote
If the paragraph already explained it, don't add a sentence that restates the same idea in different words. Move forward or stop.

## Voice & Tone

### Write Like You're Explaining a Trade Job Over Coffee
- Conversational and direct
- No corporate speak
- No filler phrases
- If you wouldn't say it out loud to a neighbour, don't write it

### Use Everyday Contractions
✅ it's, you're, don't, isn't, I'll, we're, that's, hasn't  
❌ it is, you are, do not, is not, I will, we are, that is, has not

The formal versions sound stiff and AI-generated.

### Vary Sentence Rhythm
Don't make every sentence the same length. Mix short punchy statements with longer natural explanations.

✅ "That keeps you honest. A garden ten minutes from your own front door isn't one you can do a rushed job on and forget about."

❌ "This keeps you honest. This ensures quality. This builds trust."

### Replace Abstract Claims with Physical Trade Specifics
❌ "We provide comprehensive garden solutions."  
✅ "I'll rotavate the patch, lay 100mm of topsoil, and seed it in September."

❌ "Our team delivers quality results."  
✅ "The hedge gets cut twice a year—late June and early September—so it stays thick at the base."

Use actual materials, depths, timings, and techniques. If you can't picture someone doing it, rewrite it.

## British Trade Phrasing

Use these natural British trade expressions:

- "booked in for" (not "scheduled for")
- "around 8" (not "at approximately 8am")
- "I'll be with you" (not "I'll arrive")
- "in and around [area]" (not "servicing [area]")
- "we would be happy to help with" (not "we're excited to assist")
- "chased it up" (not "followed up")
- "just up the road" (not "conveniently located nearby")
- "send over a quote" (not "provide a quotation")
- "come and have a look" (not "conduct a site visit")

## Practical Structure

### Lead with Logistics, Not Preamble
❌ "At JW Garden Services, we believe that every garden tells a story..."  
✅ "Most people ring because the garden's got away from them a bit."

### Ask Questions to Engage
Questions work. They feel natural and keep the reader involved.

✅ "How long have you been injured for?"  
✅ "Hows your driving going?"

### Keep Sign-Offs Simple
- "Thanks Josh"
- "See you Thursday"
- "Hope to hear back soon"

No "Kind regards" or "We look forward to serving you."

---

## When Writing Copy

1. Read it out loud. If it sounds like a corporate brochure, rewrite it.
2. Cut anything that sounds like you're trying to impress someone.
3. If you're using a word you wouldn't say to a friend, find a simpler one.
4. No sweeping statements. Be specific and practical.
5. Contractions everywhere. "We are" should almost always be "we're".

---

## Examples

### ❌ Bad (AI Slop)
"In today's fast-paced world, maintaining a pristine garden can be challenging. At JW Garden Services, we are passionate about transforming outdoor spaces into sustainable, beautiful havens. Our innovative approach leverages years of horticultural expertise to deliver world-class results that will elevate your property."

### ✅ Good (Natural Voice)
"Most people ring because the garden's got away from them a bit. I'll come and have a look, ask how you actually use the space, and send over a written quote with the costs broken down. No obligation."

---

## Testing Your Copy

Before committing any new copy, ask:

1. Would I say this to a customer on the phone?
2. Does it sound like a real person, or a press release?
3. Have I used any banned words or phrases?
4. Are my contractions natural?
5. Does the rhythm feel conversational?

If any answer is wrong, rewrite it.
